Giant Peelable Mango Gummy Recipe

These Giant Peelable Mango Gummies might be one of the most satisfying candies I’ve ever made!

Each gummy looks like a giant mango, but the best part is that you can actually peel off the gummy “skin” before eating it. The outside is soft, bouncy, and chewy, while the inside has a completely different texture—it’s tender, stretchy, slightly sticky, and almost melts in your mouth.

And unlike many homemade gummies that are mostly flavored with extracts, these are made with real mango purée and fresh lemon juice, so they actually taste like mango.

They’re giant, squishy, fruity, and ridiculously fun to peel!

Why You’ll Love These Giant Mango Gummies

  • 🥭 Made with real mango purée
  • 🍋 Fresh lemon juice makes the mango flavor brighter
  • ✨ Actually peelable!
  • 🍬 Two completely different gummy textures in one candy
  • 🤏 Soft, squishy, stretchy, and satisfying to play with
  • 🧡 Each gummy looks like a giant mango
  • 🎉 A fun homemade candy for parties, gifts, or videos

Why I Made These

I’ve seen so many peelable gummies online, and I’ve always thought mango would be the PERFECT fruit to turn into one.

A mango already has a soft, juicy center and a thin peel on the outside. So instead of simply making a mango-shaped gummy, I wanted to recreate that experience with candy.

The tricky part was figuring out how to make two layers that were different enough to actually peel apart.

For the inside, I cooked mango purée with tapioca starch, corn syrup, sugar, and butter until it transformed into a thick, slightly translucent, slime-like mixture. Once cooled, it becomes incredibly soft, stretchy, and chewy.

For the outside, I made a mango gelatin gummy that is firmer and bouncier.

Put them together, and you get a giant mango gummy with a thin chewy shell that can actually be peeled away from its soft mango center.

And yes—you can eat both parts!

Ingredients

This recipe makes approximately 6 giant mango gummies, depending on the size of your silicone mango molds.

Mango Purée

  • 3 large ripe mangoes, peeled and pitted
    (Frozen mango chunks also work.)
  • Juice of 1 lemon

You will need 700 g mango purée total:

  • 300 g for the mango gummy filling
  • 400 g for the peelable gummy skin

Mango Gummy Filling

  • 300 g mango purée
  • 50 g granulated sugar
  • 180 g light corn syrup
  • 30 g unsalted butter
  • 50 g tapioca starch
  • 50 g cold water

Peelable Mango Gummy Skin

  • 63 g unflavored gelatin powder
  • 240 g (1 cup) cold water
  • 400 g mango purée
  • 360 g granulated sugar
  • 280 g light corn syrup
  • A few drops of orange food coloring, optional

Equipment

  • Digital kitchen scale
  • Blender
  • Nonstick saucepan
  • Silicone spatula
  • Small mixing bowl
  • Silicone mango molds
  • Large syringe or food-safe injector
  • Measuring cups and spoons

How to Make Giant Peelable Mango Gummies

1. Make the Mango Purée

Peel the mangoes, remove the pits, and cut the flesh into chunks.

You can also use frozen mango chunks if fresh mangoes aren’t available. Let them thaw before blending.

Place the mango pieces and the juice of 1 lemon into a blender. Blend until completely smooth.

Measure out 700 g of mango purée.

Set aside:

  • 300 g for the soft mango filling
  • 400 g for the gummy skin

The lemon juice not only adds a little tartness, but also helps brighten the naturally sweet mango flavor.

2. Make the Soft Mango Gummy Filling

Add 300 g mango purée, 50 g granulated sugar, 180 g light corn syrup, and 30 g unsalted butter to a nonstick saucepan.

Heat while stirring until the butter has completely melted and everything is evenly combined.

In a small bowl, combine 50 g tapioca starch and 50 g cold water. Stir until completely smooth.

While continuously stirring the mango mixture, slowly pour in the tapioca starch slurry.

Cook over medium heat, stirring constantly with a silicone spatula.

At first, the mixture will look like an opaque yellow liquid.

Keep stirring.

And stirring.

And stirring.

After about 15–20 minutes, something very satisfying happens—the mixture will gradually become thicker, stretchier, and slightly translucent.

Eventually, it should look almost like a pot of bright yellow slime.

That’s exactly what we want!

3. Mold the Mango Filling

Divide the warm mango mixture between the cavities of your silicone mango molds.

This will become the soft center of each giant mango gummy, so don’t completely fill the molds.

Transfer the molds to the refrigerator and allow the mango filling to cool and firm up completely.

Once firm, carefully remove each mango-shaped filling from the mold.

Keep them nearby while you prepare the gummy skin.

4. Bloom the Gelatin

Sprinkle 63 g gelatin powder evenly over the surface of 240 g (1 cup) cold water.

Gently stir just enough to make sure all of the gelatin is hydrated.

Let it sit for about 10 minutes.

The gelatin will absorb the water and turn into a thick, jelly-like mass. This process is called blooming.

Don’t add dry gelatin directly to the hot mango mixture—it can easily clump.

5. Make the Mango Gummy Skin

Add 400 g mango purée, 360 g granulated sugar, and 280 g light corn syrup to a clean nonstick saucepan.

Heat while stirring until the mixture reaches a gentle boil.

Once it is just beginning to bubble, turn off the heat.

Add the bloomed gelatin and stir until it has completely dissolved into the hot mango mixture.

For a brighter mango-orange color, you can optionally add a few drops of orange food coloring.

The natural color of your gummies will depend on how ripe and deeply colored your mangoes are, so the food coloring is completely optional.

6. Create the First Layer of Mango “Skin”

Add a small spoonful of the warm mango gelatin mixture into the bottom of each mango mold cavity.

You only need enough to create a thin layer covering the bottom.

Place the molds in the refrigerator for about 30 minutes, or until this first layer has set.

This thin layer will eventually become one side of our peelable mango skin.

7. Add the Mango Filling

Remove the molds from the refrigerator.

Place one prepared mango filling into each cavity, directly on top of the set gummy layer.

Try to center the filling so that there is a little space between the filling and the walls of the mold.

That empty space is important because we need the liquid gummy mixture to flow around the filling and create a complete outer shell.

8. Close the Molds

Place the top half of each mango mold over the bottom half and secure it tightly.

Your soft mango filling should now be trapped inside the mold, surrounded by empty space that will become the gummy skin.

9. Inject the Remaining Mango Gummy Mixture

Using a large syringe or food-safe injector, slowly inject the remaining mango gelatin mixture into each mold until all of the empty space around the filling has been completely filled.

Work slowly to avoid trapping large air bubbles.

If your mango gelatin mixture starts to set while you are assembling the gummies, don’t worry!

Simply return it to low heat and warm it gently until it becomes liquid again.

Avoid boiling it again after the gelatin has been added.

10. Let the Gummies Set Overnight

Once all of the molds are filled, leave them at room temperature overnight.

This gives the gummy skin plenty of time to fully set and develop a firmer, chewier texture.

The next day, carefully open the molds and remove your giant mango gummies.

And now comes the best part…

Peel them!

The firmer mango gummy skin should separate from the soft, stretchy mango center underneath, just like peeling a giant candy mango.

You can peel the outside.

Stretch the inside.

Squish the whole mango.

Or just eat everything together!

Tips for Success

Use a Nonstick Saucepan

The mango filling becomes extremely thick and sticky as the tapioca starch cooks. A good nonstick saucepan makes both stirring and cleanup much easier.

Keep Stirring the Mango Filling

Once you add the tapioca starch slurry, continuous stirring is important.

As the starch gelatinizes, the mixture becomes very thick. Stirring keeps it cooking evenly and prevents the bottom from scorching.

It usually takes about 15–20 minutes for the mixture to transform from an opaque mango liquid into a thick, slightly translucent, slime-like consistency.

Use Cold Water for the Tapioca Starch

Always mix tapioca starch with cold water before adding it to the mango purée.

Adding dry tapioca starch directly to the hot mixture can create stubborn lumps.

Bloom the Gelatin in Cold Water

Sprinkle the gelatin over cold water and let it hydrate for about 10 minutes before adding it to the hot mango mixture.

Properly blooming the gelatin helps it dissolve evenly and gives the gummy skin a smooth texture.

Don’t Boil the Gelatin

Bring the mango, sugar, and corn syrup mixture to a gentle boil before adding the gelatin.

Then turn off the heat and stir in the bloomed gelatin.

There is no need to boil the mixture again.

Make Sure the First Gummy Layer Has Set

Before placing the mango filling into the mold, make sure the first thin layer of gummy skin has completely set.

Otherwise, the filling may sink directly through it instead of staying enclosed inside the gummy shell.

Leave Space Around the Filling

Your mango filling should be slightly smaller than the inside of the mold.

You need enough space around it for the liquid gelatin mixture to flow around the filling and form a complete outer skin.

If the Gummy Mixture Sets Too Early, Reheat It

Gelatin begins setting as it cools, so your mango gummy mixture may become thick while you are assembling six large gummies.

If this happens, simply warm it gently over low heat until it becomes pourable again.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use frozen mangoes?

Yes!

Frozen mango chunks work very well in this recipe. Thaw them first, then blend them with the lemon juice until smooth.

Depending on the variety and ripeness of the mangoes, the flavor and color may be slightly different from gummies made with fresh mangoes.

Why do I need tapioca starch in the mango filling?

Tapioca starch creates the unusual soft, stretchy, slightly sticky texture that makes the inside of these gummies different from the gelatin skin.

Without it, the inside and outside would have very similar gummy textures, and you wouldn’t get the same satisfying peelable effect.

Can I use cornstarch instead of tapioca starch?

I don’t recommend it for this recipe.

Cornstarch and tapioca starch behave differently when cooked. Tapioca starch creates a much stretchier, slightly translucent texture, which is exactly what we want for the mango center.

Cornstarch will produce a more opaque, pudding-like texture.

Why do I need corn syrup?

Light corn syrup helps give both parts of the candy a smoother, softer texture and reduces sugar crystallization.

It is especially useful in the mango center, where it helps keep the mixture soft and stretchy rather than turning hard or grainy.

Why is my mango filling taking so long to thicken?

Be patient!

Depending on your saucepan, stove, and the amount of moisture naturally present in your mangoes, it can take around 15–20 minutes of continuous cooking and stirring.

You’re looking for a dramatic texture change.

The mixture should go from an opaque yellow liquid to a very thick, stretchy, slightly translucent mixture that pulls away from the pan almost like slime.

Why isn’t my mango gummy peelable?

There are a few possible reasons.

First, make sure the inner mango filling has fully cooled and set before assembling the final gummy.

Second, the outer gelatin layer needs enough time to set and develop its own structure.

Finally, the two layers need to have distinctly different textures. The tapioca-based center should remain soft and stretchy, while the gelatin-based exterior should be firmer and bouncier.

Letting the completed gummies sit overnight gives you the best peelable texture.

Do I have to use food coloring?

No.

The gummies are naturally yellow-orange from the mango purée.

However, different mango varieties can produce very different colors. If you want that bright orange mango-candy appearance, add a few drops of orange food coloring to the gummy skin.

Can I make smaller peelable mango gummies?

Yes, but the cooking process will stay the same while the amount of filling needed for each mold will change.

Smaller gummies may also be more difficult to fill with a syringe because there is less space between the inner filling and the outer mold.

For the most dramatic peeling effect, I personally love making them GIANT!

Storage

Store the finished mango gummies in an airtight container in a cool place.

Because these gummies contain a large amount of real fruit purée and the center contains butter, they are best enjoyed fresh.

For longer storage, keep them refrigerated in an airtight container. The texture will become firmer when cold, so let the gummies sit at room temperature for a little while before peeling and eating.

Avoid leaving the gummies uncovered, as the surface can gradually dry out.

Final Thoughts

I originally wanted to make these because I thought mango would be the perfect fruit for a peelable gummy.

But I didn’t expect the inside texture to become my favorite part.

The tapioca starch transforms the mango purée into this incredibly soft, stretchy, almost slime-like candy, while the gelatin outside gives you that classic chewy gummy texture.

So every giant mango has two completely different textures.

And peeling the gummy skin off before eating it somehow makes it taste even better. 🥭

If you love mangoes, gummies, or just unnecessarily giant versions of normal-sized food, you need to try this one!
