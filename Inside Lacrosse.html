It’s championship week and the Denver Outlaws and Philadelphia Waterdogs meet Sunday at 12:30 p.m. ET to decide the 2026 PLL championship. To mark the occasion, the league dropped one probably the most artfully crafted hype videos the sport has ever seen.

Written and directed by Paul Rabil, starring Emmy, Tony and Golden Globe winner (and Oscar nominee) Jeffrey Wright, who’s a 1987 Amherst lacrosse alum. 

“Jeffrey Wright [is a] longtime friend and I've always been like, ‘Man, I want to do something with Jeffrey. I want to direct him. I want to write something for him,’” Rabil told IL in an interview last week. “He's so talented and his temperament and what I know about him and his passion for lacrosse. Like, he is a lacrosse player first, an actor second and a renaissance man in his own right. But he loves getting into the details, so you're not gonna sit with him and talk about it at a macro level. He's gonna be talking about this pick-and-roll or this switch vs. a guy maintaining his matchup by going under the screen — he’s that type of dude.”

Rabil credited Chris Blumberg, who recently became the league’s SVP of Marketing, and his team with conceiving of the playoff week theme that they were creating the biggest stage in lacrosse.

“We were illustrating the biggest stage in lacrosse,” Rabil says. “That’s a little bit provocative because you can make the argument, very cogent argument, that the biggest stage in lacrosse is the college final four. But you must provoke. And the logic is it's on ABC, so it is on the biggest platform from a distribution standpoint.”

That seems to have been all the inspiration the two-time MVP needed as he crafted a spot that hits on two tones. 

“Stage is what I thought about,” Rabil said. “We always knew we wanted to shoot him on a stage, his most modern natural habitat — he would say the field was his first stage — snd then write something that felt like he was talking about his artistry now, but is the same as the artistry on the field of our players. So I hate to sound like overly heady around this, but like what's the point of creating something if you don't like love every square millimeter of what shows up on screen?”

But for the way that Wright captures the frame as his voice filters through the smoke around the stage and the hard hits and crowd noise that emanates from the speakers, Rabil describes working with PLL’s Max Bartron on scouring clips that would work to best illustrate the stakes of the title game. From the jeers of two fans to coach Bill Tierney getting into middie Zach Currier, Rabil described having to decide whether to pick the best clips available or stick to the title game matchup, and lamented the great clips that got left on the cutting room floor.

“I’m maniacal with this stuff,” Rabil says. “Each week we were doing a little bit of process of elimination. And the thought in our head was like, best clips or finalist? Because something's gotta give there. … But like it's a championship promotion, right? You don't have to be the best all the time, you just have to be the best one that matters the most. So we ultimately kept cutting it down, cutting it down, and then on Monday night and into Tuesday morning of [last] week, we shifted some of the clips around — fortunately, the CJ Kirst piece was just brilliant, so he's always gonna be in there. Mikey Sowers' piece was last-minute shift, the pan-up of Bill Tierney in Denver, we were always gonna have that with the coach because it's just such a unbelievable shot — but yeah, there's a little bit of musical chairs. Good news is we have so many good editors and so many good shooters on game weekend that we can just comb through it.”
