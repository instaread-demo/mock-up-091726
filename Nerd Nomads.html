Are you planning to visit Ahangama in Sri Lanka? Here, we give you a complete guide to our favorite beaches, things to do, restaurants, shops, and hotels in Ahangama. We hope this Ahangama guide can help you plan your trip.

For a long time, Ahangama was a tiny village that visitors would just drive through on their way to Sri Lanka's more famous beaches like Mirissa and Weligama.

Ahangama was scarcely more than a busy bus stop and some local shops wedged between the azure blue sea and the green jungle-clad inland, pierced through by the always heavily trafficked south coast road.

Then, something happened about five years ago. The word among the island's surfers was that Ahangama had some of the island's best surf breaks. Surfers and wanna-be surfers started flocking here in ever-increasing numbers, and suddenly, Ahangama was the island's worst-kept secret.

With its newfound popularity came a rush of hip beachside diners, cafes selling slow-dripped coffee, vegan restaurants, surf shops, co-working spaces, fashionable shops, hip hostels, and cute boutique hotels. In record time, this once anonymous sleepy village transformed into Sri Lanka's hippest beach destination.

Ahangama Travel Guide

Why Ahangama? Honestly, there is a lot to like about Ahangama. If you're looking for that relaxed surf vibe reminiscent of, for instance, Bali, it has that in spades. More so than perhaps any other place in Sri Lanka, with the possible exception of Hirekitiya.

There are also many fantastic places to stay, including some stately old manors that have been transformed into lovely boutique hotels, and you'll find plenty of good restaurants serving cuisines from around the world.

While Ahangama's beaches do not rank among the island's most scenic, they have their laidback charm. It's easy to wind away the days, surf, relax beneath a palm tree, or explore the green inland on narrow roads by foot or bike.

Here, you can also glimpse the famous stilt fishermen as they clamber up their stilts with impeccable timing, just as a tourist bus grinds to a stop.

And then there are the sunsets. Few, if any, places on the south coast can match Ahangama sunsets. It is well-known for being the perfect place to watch the sun dip beneath the Indian Ocean.

This combo of hip, laid-back, cafe and surf culture, mixed with easy access to several beaches and many fabulous restaurants and hotels, is pretty unbeatable and easy to like.

Here, we give you our ultimate travel guide to Ahangama, with its top attractions/ things to do, beaches, restaurants, cafes, shops, and hotels. I hope you find it useful. Have a fantastic time in Ahangama! ♥

Ahangama's Best Beaches

Sri Lanka is blessed with many beautiful beaches. Unfortunately, Ahangama's beaches don't quite measure up to the most scenic of them. However, that is not to say they don't have a certain charm.

Kabalana Beach

Kabalana Beach is arguably the nicest beach in Ahangama and our favorite. At the right time of year (January – April) and low tide, it is a broad and 4-500-meter-long stretch of golden sand lined with palm trees.

The vibe is surf-inspired, relaxed, and friendly, with makeshift sunbeds for rent and king coconut vendors plying the beach.

Kabalana Beach is famous for its surf break, The Rock, which is considered one of the South Coast's absolute best breaks for more advanced surfers. But it's also a popular and safe beach for beginners to catch their first waves in the whitewater.

In the afternoon, the locals come to the beach for some soccer. If you fancy a game, then more players are always warmly welcomed.  Or you can join the volleyball crowd for a game at one of the two nets.

Most of the action takes place at the northern end of the beach. Here, local restaurants serve the usual Sri Lankan staples of Devilled Chicken and sandwiches, and the beach shack bars that hug the rock face keep the beers flowing past sunset.

As you walk south, hotels line the beach, ending at the high-end Sandhya Hotel and Mosvoll Villa.

What's The Catch?

Kabalana beach is quite sensitive to the tide, and at high tide, much of the beach can disappear with the water flowing all the way up to the sun beds. At the end of the southern monsoon season (November – December), the beach can almost disappear, with the sand having been shifted by the currents.

And while you can swim in the shallow whitewater, the currents can throw you about a bit, so don't expect to swim laps.

Ahangama Beach

When we first went in search of Ahangama Beach, we followed Google Maps and ended up completely lost. It turns out the location of Ahangama Beach has been much debated, and for a while, nobody was quite sure what stretch of the coast it was.

Today, while there is still no official Ahangama Beach, there seems to be a consensus that the beach is the small bay roughly between Le Café French Bistro Ahangama and Dream Sea Surf Camp.

The sand is soft and stretches out into the sea, and with the waves being relatively small, it's one of the better spots for swimming along this part of the coast.

Here, a dozen small restaurants hug the rock face that bends to form a small bay of golden sand lined with palm trees. This is the heart of Ahangama and arguably also the current center of Sri Lanka's hipster scene.

Most of the restaurants that perch on stilts along the rock face appear make-shift, adding to the boho surfy vibe. Most are foreign-owned, serving up all the food you might miss from home and some Sri Lankan favorites. Smoothie and poke bowls to vegan dishes, rice and curry, French pastries and bread, and tasty burgers. It might be the best food scene anywhere in Sri Lanka, albeit with prices that, unfortunately, also seem to be imported from Europe.

The vibe is chilled out, surfy, fun, and relaxed. Once evening sets, it's the perfect sunset spot to eat, relax with a beer, or socialize with friends.

What's The Catch?

The bay is small, only about 100 meters long, and can sometimes get quite crowded, especially in high season.

Secret Beach

Secret Beach is a small cove of golden sand towards the southern end of Ahangama. There are no hotels, restaurants, or even sunbeds here. This is the perfect beach to just put your towel underneath one of the trees and relax.

The cove forms a natural protection against the large waves that pound the coast, and the calm water and sandy bottom make this arguably the best beach for swimming in Ahangama. Sometimes, you can even spot a turtle swimming by!

What's The Catch?

Despite its name, the world is out; the beach is no longer much of a secret. And it doesn't take a lot of people before the small bay starts feeling a little crowded.

Our Favorite Cafes & Restaurants

Ahangama has arguably the best food scene in Sri Lanka, at least outside Colombo. Whether you're in the mood for Sri Lankan specialties, western food, or healthy options such as vegan and smoothie bowls, Ahangama will not disappoint.

You can eat on the beach watching the surfers do their thing or dine at a rooftop bar as the sun sets on the Indian Ocean.

Some of our favorite restaurants in Ahangama include:

Hotel de Uncles

Hotel de Uncles is one of the best-known watering holes in Ahangama. Its enviable location right at the northern end of Ahangama Beach makes it a popular stop for watching the sunset.

Originally a cocktail bar in Colombo before starting up in Ahangama, they know how to make a delicious cocktail. The daily two-for-one happy hour (3-6 pm) is a good deal. They also serve up some small but tasty Sri Lankan dishes.

  • Opening Hours: 11 am – 12 am
  • Web: Instagram

Crust

Perched on stilts overlooking the ocean on Ahangama Beach, this place has vibes by the bucket load. Add some tasty food, a DJ, or, on some days, live acoustic music, and you have the perfect recipe for an evening out.

Their delicious Italian-style pizzas with thin crusts draw in the crowds. However, the menu also includes dishes like falafel and Jackfruit Tacos.

On the flip side, service can be very slow sometimes, and waiting up to an hour during peak hours is not unusual.

  • Opening Hours: 10 am – 10:30 pm
  • Web: Instagram

Lighthouse

Situated right by the seashore, Lighthouse is a boutique hotel with a large green garden. It is also one of the tallest buildings in this area, and the view from the rooftop Stairway Cafe is incredible.

Here, you can have some tasty drinks and watch the sunset with a 360-degree view.

They also serve some small dishes if you're hungry, but we prefer to come for drinks before heading out for dinner.

  • Opening Hours: 1 pm – 10 pm
  • Web: www.lighthouseahangama.com

Cactus

Cactus is one of the nicest restaurants in Ahangama. Unlike many places along Ahangama Beach, it does not have that makeshift appearance. It is also one of the most popular, and getting a table around lunchtime can be tricky.

It is worth the wait for a table, though. The drinks are delicious, and there is much to choose from, from fresh juices and healthy smoothies to the “detox” drinks. The food is equally impressive, flavourful, well-prepared, and fresh, and the portions are generous.

The atmosphere here is fantastic. It is a fabulous place to socialize, relax, listen to the waves, and watch the action along Ahagama Beach.

Being very popular and having quite an extensive menu, they unfortunately tend to run out of some ingredients as the day unfolds. The supply situation in Sri Lanka is quite challenging, which makes running a restaurant like this difficult.

  • Opening Hours: 8 am – 6 pm
  • Web: Instagram

Marshmello Cafe

Marshmello Cafe serves tasty food in a breezy setting right on the beach with gorgeous views. The food is, for the most part, excellent, and the menu has a lot of options, including plenty of vegan options.

On the flip side, the service is a bit hit-and-miss. Sometimes, it's pretty good, but at other times, food can take forever to appear.

  • Opening Hours: 8 am -6 pm
  • Web: Instagram

Le Cafe Bistro

This small French restaurant is one of our favorites, and we ate here twice on our last visit. It's right by the ocean and serves tasty, homemade French cuisine, like the best French baguettes, tarts, and salads. Our favorite is probably the Beef Bourguignon and the chocolate tart for dessert. 🙂 The French owner and chef is super friendly, too.

  • Opening Hours: Sun – Fri 9 am – 9 pm (closed between 4 – 5 pm), Sat 9 am -4 pm
  • Web: Instagram

The Kip

A cozy little cafe set in the jungle just a 10-minute walk inland from Secret Beach. A quiet escape from the busier beachside, where you can sit in the green garden and enjoy freshly made food. It's a popular all-day breakfast place since they serve breakfast until 4 pm. The extensive menu focuses on healthy eating, including several vegan options. They also have a little shop that sells locally produced artisan products.

The Kip is also a very atmospheric place to come for dinner when the warm light from the lanterns lights up the garden.

The Coffee and Matcha are excellent. On the flip side, the prices are relatively high, and food portions are not the biggest.

  • Opening Hours: 08 am – 9:30 pm
  • Web: www.thekipsrilanka.com

Things To Do In Ahangama

Go Shopping

With new shops constantly opening, Ahangama is becoming one of the best places to shop in Sri Lanka, together with Galle. Here, you'll find the latest beachwear and locally made products, including jewelry, bags, and clothes.

Our favorite shops (or Maria's) in Ahangama are:

  • Studio Mukti
    Stylish, comfy clothes, accessories, and cosmetics. Their clothes are made in a small-scale factory in the south of Sri Lanka, where fair pay and safe working conditions for local workers are top priorities.
  • I Love Ceylon
    Cool, Sri Lankan and vintage-looking posters and postcards. Great souvenirs to bring back home to yourself or as a gift to friends and family. Have shops all over Sri Lanka, like Colombo, Galle, Mirissa, Ella, Arugam Bay, and Bandarawela.
  • Negombo Surf Co
    Comfy t-shirts, shorts, and trousers.
  • Daydream Clothing
    T-shirts and shorts, all in comfortable cotton material. Maria loves their slingbags and bucket hats.
  • Slow Days
    Cool shop with a hip interior design. Love the pink car inside the shop. Great clothes and accessories. Have shops in other places in Sri Lanka as well, like Mirissa, Ella, Arugam Bay, and Hiriketiya.
  • Cargills Food City
    Cargills supermarket is the best-stocked supermarket in Ahangama.
  • Tropical Essentials
    Cute, little local shop with caps, swimwear, board shorts, and t-shirts.
  • Living The Dream
    Stylish and cute little shop with cool clothes and accessories. The shop's owner, Jackie from Brazil, is super friendly.

Visit Handunugoda Tea Factory

Although Sri Lanka is famous for its Ceylon tea, Handunugoda Tea Factory is the only tea plantation on the southern coast that specializes in producing White Virgin Tea. White Virgin Tea is made using old Chinese methods where tea buds are picked most delicately and in such a way that no human hands have ever touched them.

The tea is produced in small quantities, is much sought after by tea lovers, and is naturally expensive. The tea is said to have a very high concentration of antioxidants.

Handunugoda is still, in part, a working tea plantation. But the old tea factory has been turned into a museum where you can learn about the tea-making process and see the old machines, some of which are over 140 years old.

When we arrived, we started with a walk around the green and lush tea plantation. In addition to tea, they also produce coconuts, rubber, and cinnamon.

Once our tour of the estate finished, we went inside the factory, where the guide told us about the many steps in the tea-making process. Finally, we could taste samples of many varieties of tea. There is also a shop where you can pick up some tea.

We couldn't resist buying a small pack of the White Virgin tea, just for fun. Maria is a huge tea lover, and trying something new is always fun.

Overall, we had a lovely time at the Handunugoda Tea Factory, and I think it's worth a visit if you are hanging around Ahangama. The factory tour was not as detailed and thorough as we had experienced at some other tea factories in Sri Lanka, like in Ella, but you still get a pretty good overview of the tea-making process.

The tour is free.

  • Opening Hours: 8:30 am – 4:15 pm
  • Cost: Free
  • Web: hermanteas.com

Go Surfing – Ahangama's Claim To Fame

There is no denying that surfing is where Ahangama shines. If you've never surfed in your life, I would still recommend taking the short drive to Wellighama to catch your first waves, but you'll also find some beginner-friendly whitewater on Kabalana Beach.

But for anyone with some experience and up to the professional level surfer, Ahangama has some epic spots.

Devil's Island

Devil's Island is the final surf break in Ahangama before crossing the bridge to Midigama. This is a picturesque spot with its characteristic little island, and paddling out here is quite the experience.

On a good day, you can surf some 4-6 footers up to 350 meters, but the reef gets very shallow closer to shore. It is best suited for intermediate surfers.

The coast here is lined with popular sunset spots, like Lighthouse and Abode by the Beach, so expect some spectators.

The Rock

Starting from the north is The Rock. Standing on Kabalana Beach, you'll see the wave breaking in front of the rock formation which has given it its name.

This is a powerful wave and best suited to advanced-level surfers. It's considered one of the island's best deep reef A-frame breaks. A consistent 8-10 feet short board wave that can offer some epic 400-meter rapid rides.

Beach Break

You'll find the best conditions for beginners in front of Timeless Villa on Kabalana Beach. Here, the whitewater is perfect for beginners, while conditions further out are well-suited for intermediate surfers working on their craft.

If you enter the lineup, be prepared for some mean closeouts and a steep takeoff. Waves can also get huge here. While the beach mostly disappears during summer, the surf close to shore remains beginner-friendly year-round.

Sticks

This break is known as Sticks, from the iconic traditional fishermen who famously perch upon sticks as they cast their lines here. Stick fishing is mainly a tourist attraction these days, but the sight is still pretty cool.

This rocky beach break is very popular and can get crowded at times. It is best suited for intermediate and advanced-level surfers.

Bring a short board, and on a good day, you'll be treated to some epic surf, which can take you about 350 meters. Don't surf much further in than the fishermen's sticks, as the reef gets shallow.

Marshmallows

At the northern end of Ahangama Town, you'll find Marshmallows. It's a favorite spot among the surf schools and perfect for anyone wanting to graduate from the sandy bottom surf and try their hand at some reef surf. The reef is deep and forgiving, and the waves are flat and fluffy (hence the name Marshmallows).

With a leisurely paddle out and slow rolling waves that break left and right, it's one of the best spots on the south coast for those graduating from beginner status. On the right day, it is also a great place to do longboarding.

Explore Cozy Side Streets & Lush Inland

It's not all about the beaches in Ahangama. We love to explore the green inland with its lush and green fields, gravel roads, charming old Colonial-style buildings, and cozy cafes. Whether you have a motorcycle, a bike, or walk, it makes for a pleasant break from the busy city center and the beaches.

A surprising number of stately old colonial-style mansions are dotted around the neighborhoods. Some have been restored and converted into cozy Airbnbs and boutique hotels. And every time we visit, there seem to be new cafes popping up.

Black Honey Cafe

One of our favorite inland stops is Black Honey Cafe, with its peaceful setting among palm trees overlooking a green field.

The food is excellent, and the vibe is friendly and laid back. It's also a popular place to bring a laptop and do some work. They have a cute little shop as well, where Maria bought a skirt and a T-shirt.

  • Opening Hours: 8 am – 4 pm
  • Web: Instagram

Recommended Day Trips

One of the reasons Ahangama has become so popular and why many expats have made it their home is its central location. You are less than one hour from Galle, one of Sri Lanka's major cities.

I highly recommend taking a day trip to the old Galle Fort. This once-Portuguese coastal fort town is super charming and well worth the trip. See our guide to Galle below for more on what Galle has to offer.

Where To Stay In Ahangama

Top End

Mosvold Villa
With a fantastic location next to Kabalana Beach, Mosvold Villa is arguably the top-end place to stay in Ahangama. This mid-sized luxury boutique hotel has 17 gorgeous rooms with stunning sea views.

Mosvoll Villa has that old-world charm by the bucketloads. The rooms are the very definition of classic colonial-style luxury. It is comfortable yet updated with all the modern comforts you'd expect at a five-star hotel.

The beautiful garden has a lovely swimming pool with plenty of relaxing sun loungers. The food is excellent, and the breakfast spread is legendary. Mosvold Villa is the brainchild of a fellow Norwegian philanthropist, Kurt Mosvold.

Casa Tikiri Boutique Hotel – Adults Only
Casa Tikiri is hands down our favorite place to stay in Ahangama. It may even be one of our favorite places to stay in Sri Lanka. That is how much we liked staying here!

This little boutique hotel is simply fantastic, from the stunning tropical garden with its gorgeous pool to the beautiful, cozy rooms. The Italian owners, Alice and Stefano, have succeeded in creating a truly charming hotel. We simply did not want to leave.

There are three rooms and four suites. We stayed in room number 6, one of the king rooms (23 sqm). Our room was spacious and stylishly decorated with antique furniture, high-quality bed linens, and a mosquito net of breathable cotton, should you need it (we did not). The moody interior lighting meant the room got a little dark once the sun set, but it was never a problem for us.

The room has a water dispenser with cool water, an electric water kettle, cups, and complimentary tea. The air conditioning works well, and the WiFi is stable and fast.

The partially open bathroom with large green plants looks fantastic, and the shower is excellent.

Our room had a little balcony with chairs outside to sit and enjoy the garden. The food is another highlight. Everything we tried in the restaurant was always cooked fresh, looked stunning, and tasted amazing. Breakfast is à la carte, and there are plenty of options. They also serve lunch and dinner, including authentic homemade Italian pasta. Loved it!

Once evening sets in, the lights around the property come on, and you can relax by the pool or in a hammock.

This is an adults-only hotel, perfect for those traveling alone or as a couple. Casa Tikiri is a 10-minute walk from Kabalana Beach, in a quiet residential neighborhood away from the busy main road.

Lastly, I must mention the excellent staff, especially the manager, who is super friendly and helped us arrange day trips and onward transportation. The hotel has the cutest little shop as well.

Casa Tikiri is very popular, so it is best to book in advance.

Mid Range

Kiri Palu
This 100-year-old colonial mansion was restored in 2019 and converted into a gorgeous seven-room boutique hotel. We had just booked one night, but once we arrived, we wished we could have stayed longer.

Our room was more like an apartment! The spacious bedroom had a comfortable bed, a working desk, and lots of closet space. In addition, we had a small lounge with a sofa group and a large, modern, partially open-air bathroom.

The villa is beautiful, with high wooden ceilings and an open design that lets in the natural sunlight and makes the air circulate. At the back of the villa, there is a large green garden.

Here, you'll find our favorite part of the villa, the lovely swimming pool with six sun loungers. The garden also has a small pool house with a pool table and TV.

We ate dinner here in the evening, and the food was good. Breakfast was pretty standard, but Ahangama has a lot of fancy breakfast cafes if you feel like mixing it up.

Despite being in a quiet neighborhood off the main road, you're not far from the beaches. We walked down to Ahangama Beach in about 10 minutes.

Lum Residence
Lum Residence is a relatively new (opened December 2023) mid-range hotel offering excellent value. The two rows of stylish bungalows are each divided into apartments, one downstairs and one upstairs.

We stayed on the upper floor and had a spacious apartment with high ceilings and a little balcony. The apartments downstairs have a larger garden area.

Our apartment had a small kitchen with a fridge, cutlery, and a tea kettle. Everything was new and spotlessly clean. The bed was super comfortable, and we loved the high-quality linen. Everything is well thought out with welcome small touches like USB charging by the bed.

There is a lovely swimming pool and an outdoor Yoga deck. Almost every day, they arrange some activities, from Yoga classes to barbecues and even live YouTube broadcasts. The place has a friendly, youthful surf vibe.

We had dinner here one evening, and the food and drinks were excellent. The owners are from Russia, so you'll also find some Russian specialties on the menu. The Sri Lankan staff were very friendly and spoke excellent English.

While it was set a bit away from the main road, we walked to Kabalana Beach in about 10 minutes.

All in all, we were very impressed with Lum Residence for the price and could easily have stayed longer.

The three studio rooms share the same beautiful Moroccan-inspired interior design as the cafe, are quite spacious (30sqm), and have a kitchenette and a large balcony overlooking the stunning paddy fields.

Black Honey is surrounded by nature, and the garden is a lovely place to relax or cool down with a dip in the small pool.

And then there is, of course, the cafe downstairs, which serves excellent coffee and some delicious food. Almost every day, something is happening here, like Yoga classes, breathwork workshops, barbeques, ping pong tournaments, or surf skate lessons.

Staying here feels like a peaceful oasis compared to the busy beachside, yet you're just a few minutes away from the action.

Budget

 The Well
This beautiful hostel is just a few minutes walk from the Ahangama center, yet it seems miles away from the busy beachside with its green and lush “jungle” setting. The vibe here is relaxed, friendly, and social. It is the perfect place for meeting like-minded travelers, joining one of their many Yoga classes, or just chilling by the gorgeous swimming pool.

Both dormitory rooms (mixed and female only) and private double rooms are available. The shared showers and bathrooms are kept spotlessly clean, and the beds are super comfy. With its friendly staff, good vibes, and affordable prices, we think The Well is the best budget accommodation in Ahangama.

Best Time To Visit

Like the rest of the south coast, the best time to visit Ahangama is generally from December through March. Although the shoulder season of April can still provide many days of nice weather, you can expect some more rain as we head into the monsoon season.

How To Get There

Ride The Train or Bus

Ahangama is right on the southern train line, so one option is to take a train from Colombo. The train ride from Colombo to Ahangama takes about 3 hours. The train ride down the south coast is beautiful, and we've done it several times. However, the trains from Colombo heading south typically run early in the day, so if you fly in late, you must spend a night in Negombo or Colombo first.

The best way to book train tickets in Sri Lanka is through 12Go.

If you're feeling adventurous, you can also take the bus. However, be aware that Sri Lankan buses are notorious for their reckless driving, and they do get very crowded at times. We tend to avoid them whenever possible.

Hire A Car And Driver

The easiest and fastest way to get to Ahangama is to pre-book a taxi before you arrive, for instance with 12Go or KiwiTaxi. Then the car and driver will be waiting for you when you land.

You can also use ride-sharing apps like PickMe and Uber. These apps are mainly used in Colombo, but if you are starting from there, you should have no trouble finding a driver.
