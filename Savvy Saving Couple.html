Homemade Pumpkin Spice Sauce with Sweetened Condensed Milk

The Ultimate DIY Fall Coffeehouse Sauce & learn how to use it in a delicious pumpkin spice latte! Let’s be honest—the moment there’s even a slight hint of a breeze outside, our brains immediately demand pumpkin spice everything. But instead of shelling out six bucks a cup at the drive-thru or wrestling with watery syrups that taste like a melted candle, we are making the good stuff today. This rich, velvety pumpkin spice sauce uses real pumpkin, cozy autumn spices, and sweetened condensed milk for a café-quality pumpkin sauce you can make in under 10 minutes.

Why You’ll Fall Head Over Heels For This Recipe

Look, no shade to the Bucks’, but their standard sauce doesn’t actually contain real pumpkin. Gasp. We are fixing that injustice right now by adding my fave fall staple which happens to be good old canned pumpkin -yum!

  • Real Ingredients Only: We’re using actual canned pumpkin puree because, call us crazy, pumpkin spice ought to feature actual pumpkin.
  • The Magic of Condensed Milk: Unlike watery coffee syrups, sweetened condensed milk gives this sauce a luscious, thick, spoon-coating texture that hugs your espresso.
  • Zero Artificial Aftertaste: Enhanced with an extra kick of cinnamon, pure vanilla, and a sneaky pinch of salt to balance the sweetness so it tastes like a high-end artisanal sauce.

The Ingredients

Here is everything you need to create your “autumn in a cup” right on your stovetop:

  • 1 can (14 oz) Sweetened condensed milk (the secret weapon)
  • 1/2 cup Pumpkin puree (Make sure it’s 100% pure pumpkin, not the pre-spiced pie mix unless you enjoy sugar-shock)
  • 1/2 tablespoon Ground cinnamon (because we like things bold & spicy)
  • 1 teaspoon Pure vanilla extract
  • 1/4 teaspoon Salt (crucial—trust us, it keeps the sweetness grounded)
  • 1/2 teaspoon Ground ginger
  • 1/4 teaspoon Ground nutmeg
  • 1/4 teaspoon Ground allspice

Step-by-Step Instructions: The Sauce

  1. Whisk: In a small saucepan over medium-low heat, combine your sweetened condensed milk, pumpkin puree, cinnamon, ginger, nutmeg, allspice, and salt. Whisk it like you mean it until it’s completely smooth and looks like liquid autumn.
  2. Simmer: Bring the mixture to a very gentle, low bubble. Let it hang out on the heat, stirring constantly, for 3 to 5 minutes. Don’t skip this! It cooks out the raw edge of the pumpkin and lets those spices deeply flirt with the dairy.
  3. Grand Finale: Pull the pan off the heat and stir in your pure vanilla extract. Your kitchen is going to smell like a Yankee Candle factory exploded, and it’s glorious- haha!!
  4. Chilling: Let the sauce cool completely, then transfer it to a clean glass jar. Stash it in the fridge where it will happily live for up to 2 weeks (though let’s be real, it won’t last that long).

How to Make a Pumpkin Spice Latte at Home

You’ve got the goodness. Now, lets turn it into a PSL.

  1. Scoop the Sauce: Spoon 2 tablespoons of your homemade pumpkin spice sauce right into the bottom of your favorite mug.
  2. Add the Espresso: Pull a double shot of espresso (or brew about 1/3 cup of strong coffee) directly over the sauce.
  3. Stir: Whisk the espresso and sauce together until it forms a gorgeous, creamy, aromatic paste.
  4. Pour the Milk: Heat and froth about 3/4 cup of your favorite milk (whole milk, oat milk, and almond milk all work here) and pour it right on top.
  5. Be a Barista: Top with lots of whipped cream, caramel drizzle, and some pumpkin spice finishing sugar from McCormick or pumpkin spice.

Pro-Tips for Maximum Coziness

  • Too Cold Out of the Fridge? Since the base is condensed milk, the sauce gets nice and thick when chilled. If it’s too thick to mix into your coffee straight from the fridge, just pop your mug’s serving in the microwave for 10 seconds first.
  • Not Just for Coffee: Drizzle this liquid gold over vanilla ice cream, oatmeal, pancakes, or French toast. We won’t judge if you eat it straight off the spoon.

Homemade Pumpkin Spice Sauce with Sweetened Condensed Milk

Make your own café-quality Pumpkin Spice Sauce with Sweetened Condensed Milk right at home in under 10 minutes! This rich, velvety syrup uses real pumpkin puree, cozy autumn spices, and vanilla for a thick, spoon-coating texture that rivals the coffeehouse favorite. Easily mix a few tablespoons into espresso and frothed milk for the ultimate homemade Pumpkin Spice Latte (PSL)—this recipe yields about 12 servings.
Prep Time 5 minutes minutes
Cook Time 5 minutes minutes
Total Time 10 minutes minutes
Course Dessert, drink
Cuisine American, cafe-style
Servings 12 servings
Calories 112cal
Author Amy Desrosiers
Cost $4

Equipment

  • small saucepan
  • Whisk
  • storage container

Ingredients

  • 14 ounces sweetened condensed milk
  • ½ cup Pumpkin puree (100% pure canned pumpkin, not pie mix)
  • ½ tablespoon ground cinnamon
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • ¼ teaspoon salt
  • ½ teaspoon ground ginger
  • ¼ teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • ¼ teaspoon ground all-spice

Instructions

  • In a small saucepan over medium-low heat, whisk together the sweetened condensed milk, pumpkin puree, cinnamon, ginger, nutmeg, allspice, and salt until completely smooth.
  • Bring the mixture to a gentle, low bubble. Stir constantly for 3 to 5 minutes to cook out the raw pumpkin flavor and let the spices infuse into the dairy.
  • Remove the pan from the heat and stir in the pure vanilla extract until fully incorporated.
  • Let the sauce cool completely, then transfer it to a clean glass jar or airtight container. Refrigerate for up to 2 weeks.

Notes

Nutritional values may vary and are meant to be a guide.
How to Make a Pumpkin Spice Latte (PSL)
  1. Spoon 2 tablespoons of the pumpkin spice sauce into the bottom of your favorite mug.
  2. Pour a double shot of hot espresso (or 1/3 cup of strong brewed coffee) directly over the sauce.
  3. Whisk vigorously until smooth and creamy.
  4. Pour in 3/4 cup of your favorite heated and frothed milk (whole milk, oat milk, or almond milk).
  5. Top with whipped cream and a dusting of cinnamon, if desired.
