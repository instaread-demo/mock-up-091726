    Ways to get around the Philippines (Transportation options)

    Published: Aug 3, 2026 · Updated: Sep 15, 2026 by Tammilee · This post may contain affiliate links

    Philippines Transportation ranges from commercial airline flights to Jeepneys, Trike Taxis, and Water Taxis along with buses and more.

    Transportation in the Philippines varies depending on where you are trying to go and how much time you want to take to get there.

    Airports

    Depending on where you're headed, you may not be routing through Manila or Cebu at all. Here are the key gateways to know:

    • Ninoy Aquino International Airport (MNL) - Manila, the country's main international hub
    • Mactan-Cebu International Airport (MCIA) - Cebu, second-largest hub with a growing number of direct international routes
    • Clark International Airport (CRK) - Pampanga, a popular alternative entry point to Manila that's often less congested
    • Kalibo International Airport (KLO) and Godofredo P. Ramos Airport (MPH) - the two main gateways for Boracay
    • Francisco Bangoy International Airport (DVO) - Davao, for southern Mindanao
    • Puerto Princesa International Airport (PPS) - Palawan
    • Iloilo International Airport (ILO) - Iloilo and the Western Visayas

    If you're flying into the Philippines, you will most likely land in Manila and then transfer to your final destination, though flying into a secondary gateway can sometimes save you a leg of travel depending on where you're headed.

    For getting around domestically, the main carriers are Philippine Airlines (full service), Cebu Pacific (low cost), and AirAsia Philippines (low cost). One thing to check is the cost for baggage on each flight and how often flights travel to your final destination.

    12Go provides the opportunity to search your flights and see all of the options available.

    Buses

    Buses are a popular way to get between cities within the Philippines. Make sure you check to see if you will need a transfer to a ferry or domestic flight to reach your destination.

    12GO allows you to search your departure city and destination and see all of the options available for the day you want to travel.

    There are different bus travel classes and seat types so make sure to check not only the price but also the comfort level you want to experience on the bus ride.

    Air-conditioned buses are listed as standard/regular. It can be hot, humid, and bumpy on a bus ride so we highly suggest going for an air conditioned bus with comfortable seats.

    There are also options for first class and deluxe buses along with vip and sleeper buses. If youa re traveling a long distance the sleeper bus is a great way to grab a nap or watch a movie.

    Jeepneys, Trike Taxis

    Jeepneys are one of the most recognizable symbols of the Philippines, originally built from US military jeeps left behind after World War II. They've evolved into brightly decorated, customized vehicles that run along fixed routes through cities and towns. Each jeepney has its route posted on the side, so you'll want to check that before hopping on. Fares are cheap, and you typically pass your fare up to the driver through the other passengers, so don't be shy about participating in the hand-off.

    Don't skip the chance to snap a photo of one. The paint jobs, decorations, and personality on these things make for one of the most iconic shots you can get in the Philippines.

    You'll also start seeing modern e-jeeps and m-jeeps on the road now, cleaner, more comfortable versions of the classic jeepney, and some even accept cashless payment. They're part of a broader push to modernize the older diesel fleet, so don't be surprised if the jeepney you flag down looks a little different from the postcard version.

    Tricycles are three-wheeled motorcycles with a sidecar attached, and they're everywhere, especially for short trips in neighborhoods, provincial towns, and areas that jeepneys and buses don't cover. Unlike jeepneys, they don't run fixed routes. Just tell the driver where you're headed and agree on a fare before you get in. In many areas, the local government sets standard short-distance rates, but it's worth confirming the price upfront, especially for longer trips or as a visitor.

    In smaller towns and more rural stops on your itinerary, don't be surprised if the tricycle is essentially the only transportation option available. It's worth having small bills on hand since cash is standard for both jeepneys and tricycles.

    Water Taxis & Ferries

    If you are planning to island hop you will most likely travel on a ferry or small outrigger boat between stops.

    With 7,000+ islands in the Philippines it is common to travel via boat to get to many destinations.

    You can book ferry tickets on 12Go to secure a confirmed seat in advance of your travels. This gives you the opportunity to pick which type of service you want for your trip.

    If you are traveling during the busy season or major holidays, it is highly suggested to book as far in advance as possible to make sure you can get a cabin and comfortable transportation.

    Some of the major ferry operators to know are 2GO Travel and OceanJet, both of which run routes connecting many of the larger islands.

    For shorter hops, especially getting to smaller islands or beach access points, you'll likely be on a banca (a traditional outrigger boat) rather than a large ferry. Larger RORO (roll-on/roll-off) ferries are more common for longer routes and are your option if you're bringing a vehicle along.

    We took a water taxi to Puerto Princesa Subteranean River National Park and it worked out great.

    Grab - Rideshare/Ride-hailing

    Uber and Lyft are not a thing in the Philippines. Grab is the go-to ride-hailing app in the Philippines and it's one of the easiest ways to get around cities like Manila and Cebu. You book through the app, see your fare upfront, and pay cashless, no haggling over price and no worrying about a meter "not working."

    If you're heading out of the main tourist areas, keep in mind Grab coverage thins out fast once you leave the bigger cities, so don't count on it in smaller towns or more rural stops on your itinerary.

    If you'd rather stick with a traditional taxi, they're still easy to find in Manila and Cebu, just make sure the driver turns on the meter before you take off, or agree on a flat price before you get in.

    Trains: LRT, MRT & BRT

    If you're spending time in Metro Manila, the LRT and MRT lines are worth knowing about. They run along major routes through the city and can be one of the fastest ways to skip traffic, especially during rush hour. Just be ready for crowds, mornings and evenings get packed.

    Cebu City also has its own Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) system if you're spending time there.

    One thing to note: this is city transit only. The Philippines doesn't have a long-distance train network connecting cities or islands, so for that you're back to flights, buses, and ferries.

    If you plan on riding the rails more than once or twice, grab a reloadable Beep card at any station, it saves you from waiting in the single-ticket line every time and works across LRT, MRT, and some bus lines too.

    Getting around the Philippines takes a little planning, but that's part of what makes travel here so memorable. Whether you're catching a flight between islands, settling in for a scenic ferry ride, or squeezing onto a jeepney for the first time, each mode of transportation gives you a different slice of what everyday life looks like here.

    A few things that make getting around the Philippines easier: keep small bills and coins on hand, since cash is standard for jeepneys, tricycles, and many buses. Always confirm your fare before you get in, whether that's insisting a taxi driver turns on the meter or agreeing on a price with a tricycle driver upfront. And it's worth keeping a couple of emergency numbers saved in your phone just in case: police at 117 and medical emergencies at 911.

    Our best advice is to build in extra time, keep small bills on hand, and stay flexible. Schedules can shift, especially around weather and holidays, and the best experiences often come from just going with it.

The program helps to streamline over 730,000 routes and thousands of operators that can be used to book travel around the Philippines and Asia.
