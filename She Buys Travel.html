Washington’s San Juan Islands Solo Travel Guide

By

Estelle Rogers

The San Juan Islands are my ultimate Pacific Northwest escape. Exploring these quiet roads on my own feels like slipping into a cozy sweater passed down from a favorite aunt: perfectly comfortable and somehow, already familiar. Across San Juan, Lopez and Orcas islands, I discovered slow-paced adventures, inspiring landscapes and restorative experiences that make this the perfect, peaceful corner for a solo getaway.

Highlights

  • Cozy Stays & Secret Reading Spots: Learn about my favorite island lodgings—from a welcoming farm to cozy cabins—and the most serene coastal benches to settle in with a good book.
  • Solo Dining & Island Treasures: Discover the best independent boutiques, local bookstores and unforgettable culinary finds (including a life-changing pastrami baguette).
  • Safe & Seamless Solo Travel: Master practical tips for navigating the WSDOT ferry system, personal safety habits and packing essentials for a stress-free journey.

Part 1: Preparing for Your Solo Journey

Speed limits range from 25 mph to a maximum of 45 mph, perfect for letting life’s worries drift away during an unhurried drive. Along winding roads through fields and forested countryside, my heartbeat slows as street signs reading Lazy Lane, Carefree Avenue and Quiet Place come into view.

Safety Tips for Solo Female Travelers

Safety stays at the forefront of my mind. Before leaving my lodging for the day, I run through a mental checklist of everything I need for my outing:

  • The Essentials: I always carry an external charger in case my phone battery dies, and I double-check that I have my reading glasses, sunglasses and a hat. Wearing a hat while sitting or reading outside blocks the direct sun, something I appreciate more each year.
  • The Route: I also review directions to my next destination while sitting in my car before leaving my lodging. That habit gives me peace of mind before driving on unfamiliar roads.
  • The Extras: I carry a couple of collapsible bags in my purse because shopping opportunities pop up almost anywhere. Nanobag remains my personal favorite and continues to hold up well.
  • Packing Tip: Packing extra shoes, socks, a lightweight rain poncho and collapsible hiking poles for the San Juan Islands helps tremendously. Even easy trails can include elevation changes, uneven ground and the islands always bring unexpected weather.

Part 2: Getting There & Navigating the Ferry System

Mastering the WSDOT ferry system and nearby mainland towns requires proactive planning:

  • WSDOT Ferry Logistics: Start with the First-Time Riders page. Vehicle passengers need both a reservation and a ticket, with tickets released about two months before each seasonal schedule.
  • Mainland Gateways: Historic, chain-free La Conner is famous for spring flower fields. In Anacortes, Ship Harbor Inn offers a two-minute drive to early ferry lines.
  • Alternative Transit: Kenmore Air flies to the islands, but limited local car rentals make advance planning essential.

Part 3: The Island-by-Island Guides

1. San Juan Island

Where to Stay

  • Snug Harbor Resort and Marina : Solo travelers feel comfortable in these cozy, fully equipped cabins. An on-site coffee shop, gift shop and essentials store share a building with the front desk, making it easy to settle in without leaving the property.

Dining & Local Shopping

  • Dining Alone: The islands are a diner’s paradise. On San Juan Island, Duck Soup and Downriggers are standout restaurants where I have mastered the art of dining alone, even at the fanciest tables.
  • Local Shops: I walked into Fern, a boutique filled with glass-on-metal art by Alisha Merrick, and walked out wearing a smooth, shiny seafoam green glass ring. It pairs beautifully with the savory souvenirs I carried home from a few blocks away: grinders filled with San Juan Island Sea Salt’s signature pepper-and-salt blend.

Literary Stops & Best Places to Read

  • Serendipity Used Bookstore: Invites visitors to browse towering shelves, including an entire section of local authors and settle in to read a few chapters.
  • Griffin Bay Bookstore & San Juan Public Library: Excellent local spots worth visiting.
  • Fairweather Park, Friday Harbor: The benches overlooking the marina hide in plain sight, creating a quiet spot to settle in with a good book for ferry watching, reading and relaxing.
  • Lime Kiln Café, Roche Harbor: Famous for fresh doughnuts made each morning, the café sits at the end of the dock, where busy marina activity unfolds between pages.
  • Lime Kiln Point State Park (Whale Watch Park): Settle onto a bench beside the 107-year-old historic lighthouse and enjoy sweeping views across Haro Strait while watching for whales.
  • Jackson Beach: With 2 miles of sand and driftwood, this shoreline invites readers to linger beside the gentle rhythm of waves lapping against the shore. It offers a quiet place to sit, read and enjoy the sweeping views.
  • Zylstra Lake Preserve: This 300-acre protected wildlife habitat welcomes birdwatchers, walkers and readers alike. Follow the 2-mile trail around the lake or claim the overlook bench and take in the peaceful surroundings.

2. Lopez Island

Where to Stay

  • The Edenwild Boutique Inn : Edenwild Boutique Inn sits on a large corner lot in the heart of downtown Lopez, just a five-minute walk from shops, restaurants and the historic public library. Weather permitting, owner Anthony lights the outdoor communal fire pit for guests.

Dining

  • Blue Heron Bistro: A standout restaurant on Lopez Island that makes a wonderful stop for solo diners.

Literary Stops & Best Places to Read

  • Lopez Bookshop & Lopez Island Library: Perfect neighborhood stops to pick up your next read and explore local history.
  • Odlin County Park: Nestled at the end of a short, winding road, this quiet cove welcomed me, my picnic lunch and my book. When I arrived, the only other vehicle belonged to a gentleman who, as if on cue, was enjoying a leisurely book during his lunch hour.
  • Spencer Spit State Park: Locals know the pavilion at the end of the lagoon as one of the island’s most serene spots, though plenty of picnic tables provide inviting places to settle in.
  • Shark Reef Sanctuary: A small parking lot accommodates only a handful of vehicles, so I recommend arriving early. The trail begins on a wooden boardwalk before winding to the water’s edge, where a scenic dream bench rewards the walk with sweeping views overlooking the Salish Sea.

3. Orcas Island

Where to Stay

  • Pebble Cove Farm : Staying on an active farm surrounded by a thriving garden delights the senses. Upon arrival, friendly farm animals make their way onto the front lawn to greet guests. Before I know it, I’m petting goats, a large pig and a small pony. Beyond the warm animal welcome, guests also enjoy access to the working garden, a private cove, water toys, safety vests and Adirondack chairs around the fire pit with tranquil waterfront views.

Wellness, Dining & Unexpected Treats

  • Alchemy Sound Baths: Lying on my back in the darkened room, I listened as Andreia from Attune played crystal bowls of various sizes, ringing and tapping them to create soothing vibrations and calming tones. The gentle sounds helped quiet my mind and regulate my nervous system. The experience ranked among the highlights of my trip and fit perfectly with my quest for peaceful solo activities. I will never forget this first sound bath.
  • Fine Dining: Houlme and Matia Kitchen are exceptional standout restaurants for solo travelers.
  • The Standout Sandwich: In a surprise twist, one standout dish came from an unexpected find. Callum at Orcas Bakery created a seemingly simple yet mouthwatering pastrami baguette with house pickles, stone-ground mustard and Swiss cheese. I returned to the bakery for the next four days in pursuit of another, only to discover they had sold out each time. That sandwich will remain a palate memory for a very long time.

Literary Stops & Best Places to Read

  • Darvill’s Book Store & Orcas Island Public Library: Wonderful spots to browse and find local inspiration.
  • The Soaking Pools at Doe Bay Resort, Olga: A crashing waterfall tumbles just feet from the warm soaking pools, creating an idyllic setting to settle in, read and soak away the day. The outdoor soaking pools overlook the bay and waterfall (reservations required).
  • Mount Constitution in Moran State Park, Olga: Sweeping 360-degree views from 2,400 feet made it difficult to keep my attention on the words in my book.
  • Judd Cove Preserve: After a short hike, the preserve reveals the historic remnants of Gregg’s Lime Kiln, dating to 1888, along with a quiet bench overlooking the cove.
  • Obstruction Pass State Park: Perhaps the biggest little hike of my trip, this well-marked trail leads to the water’s edge.

Part 4: The San Juan Literary Community

The San Juan Islands nurture a thriving community of writers:

  • Diverse Authors: Local talent ranges from mystery novelists D.W. Ulsterman and Laura Gayle to historical chronicler Boyd C. Pratt and essayist Iris Graville.
  • Creative Community: Groups like the Island Verse Literary Collective spark inspiration through weekly workshops, open mic nights and shared spoken word.
  • Retreats & Events: Creative growth flourishes at gatherings like Write Doe Bay, the Friday Harbor Writing Weekend and the San Juan Island Artists’ Studio Tour.

Frequently Asked Questions About the San Juan Islands

How far are the San Juan Islands from Seattle?

The drive from Seattle to Anacortes takes approximately 1 hour and 45 minutes, depending on traffic. From Anacortes, the ferry ride is approximately 1 hour and 45 minutes. Always consult WSDOT for ferry updates.

Do I need a passport?

No. The San Juan Islands are in the northernmost part of Washington and border Canada.

Do I need a car to visit or is Uber available?

Walk-ons are welcome on the ferry. It is advisable to bring your own vehicle if you want to fully explore the islands. Uber does not operate on the San Juan Islands.

Will I see a whale when I visit?

Whale sightings are not guaranteed. Odds are highest in the summer and early fall.

Which island is the safest for solo female travelers?

All three of the islands are equally safe, warm and welcoming. Personal responsibility is every traveler’s best defense against danger.
